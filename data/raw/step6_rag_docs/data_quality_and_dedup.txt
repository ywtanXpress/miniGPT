Data quality strongly affects language model training.
If the corpus contains spam, corrupted text, or heavy duplication, the model can waste capacity memorizing artifacts instead of learning robust patterns.

Deduplication matters for two reasons.
First, exact duplicates overrepresent certain documents and skew the training distribution.
Second, near duplicates can leak repeated phrasing into many shards even when the text is not byte-for-byte identical.

One common strategy is exact deduplication with hashing.
Another is approximate deduplication with locality-sensitive signatures such as SimHash.
Approximate methods help catch documents that differ by only a few edits, formatting changes, or repeated boilerplate.

Quality filters are also useful.
Examples include minimum length, maximum noise ratio, and repeated-line heuristics.
Those checks are not perfect, but they remove many low-value pages from web-style corpora.

If someone asks why deduplication matters, a concise answer is:
"Deduplication improves data diversity, reduces overcounting of repeated text, and helps the model generalize better."
